[[req_recom]]
=== Field reference

The tables below list the most important fields, grouped by the table they go in. Click a field name to see its official definition. Use the field name as your column header: the MDT then maps it automatically.

Which table a field goes in depends on how its value varies:

* It differs between samples: a column in the *Samples* table.
* It differs between OTUs: a column in the *Taxonomy* table.
* It is the same for the whole dataset: a row in the *Study* table, or a default value in step 2 (see <<map_defaults>>).

The tables below show each field where it most often goes. Put each field in one table only (see <<file_rules>>).

The *Level* column shows how important each field is. The levels follow GBIF's https://techdocs.gbif.org/en/data-publishing/data-quality-recommendations#data-quality-requirements-for-occurrences[data quality requirements for occurrences]. For some fields this guide is stricter than GBIF, never looser.

* *Required:* GBIF or the MDT will not process your data without it.
* *Strongly recommended:* makes your data much more useful, for example through better name matching and more precise locations.
* *Share if available:* adds value if you have it.

[[all_terms]]
==== Other fields you can add

The templates and the tables below contain only the most important fields. You can add any other term from the following lists, as an extra column in your Samples or Taxonomy table, as a value in the Study table, or in step 2 (see <<map_terms>>):

* Darwin Core terms for occurrences and sampling events, listed with their definitions in GBIF's https://rs.gbif.org/core/dwc_occurrence_2025-07-10.xml[Occurrence core] and https://rs.gbif.org/core/dwc_event_2025-07-10.xml[Event core]. The Event terms describe the sample, for example term:dwc[samplingEffort], term:dwc[habitat], term:dwc[locality] or term:dwc[minimumDepthInMeters]. See also the https://dwc.tdwg.org/terms/[Darwin Core quick reference guide].
* DNA terms from GBIF's https://rs.gbif.org/extension/gbif/1.0/dna_derived_data_2024-07-11.xml[DNA derived data extension]. Most of them come from https://genomicsstandardsconsortium.github.io/mixs/[MIxS], the checklists of the Genomic Standards Consortium, which the nucleotide archives GenBank, ENA and DDBJ (see <<insdc>>) also use to describe samples. Using these terms means your samples are described the same way on GBIF and in the archives. Examples: term:mixs[samp_vol_we_dna_ext] (volume of water filtered) and term:mixs[nucl_acid_ext] (DNA extraction method).

Measurements that have no term, such as salinity or temperature, can be published as measurements in step 2 (see <<map_terms>>).

[[stable_ids]]
==== Filled in by the MDT

The MDT makes one occurrence for each OTU in each sample where it has at least one read (zero reads are *not* treated as absence).

[NOTE]
====
*You do not supply these fields.* The MDT automatically adds them to every occurrence. Examples are from the practice dataset (see <<example_data>>).

* term:dwc[occurrenceID]: constructed from the sample ID, a colon, and OTU ID. Example: `120_2022-04-20:MiFish_UE-asv0001`
* term:dwc[eventID]: constructed from the sample ID. Example: `120_2022-04-20`
* term:dwc[basisOfRecord]: automatically set as `MATERIAL_SAMPLE`, because each record is based on DNA from a physical sample (such as water or soil), not on an observed or collected organism
* term:dwc[organismQuantity]: number of reads of the OTU in the sample. Example: `384076`
* term:dwc[organismQuantityType]: automatically set as `DNA sequence reads`
* term:dwc[sampleSizeValue]: automatically calculated (and added) as total number of reads in the sample (all OTUs). Example: `1897112`
* term:dwc[sampleSizeUnit]: automatically set as `DNA sequence reads`
* term:dwc[taxonID]: constructed as `ASV:` and the MD5 checksum of the sequence. Example: `ASV:b68f65a31a69fb168c1e27439a4456e2`
====

This covers GBIF's required term occurrenceID and the strongly recommended basisOfRecord, organismQuantity and organismQuantityType.

[[table-02]]
==== Taxonomy table: one row per OTU

[[which_name]]
*Which name to give:* share every OTU, and give it the most specific identification you trust in term:dwc[scientificName].

* Identified to species: the species name, for example _Sprattus sprattus_.
* Identified only to a higher rank: the name at that rank, for example the family Ammodytidae. Do this even if you only know the kingdom (Animalia) or the domain (Bacteria). Give the rank in term:dwc[taxonRank].
* Not identified at all: leave term:dwc[scientificName] empty. Do not write "unknown", "unidentified" or "NA".

[cols="3,2,3,4",options="header"]
|===
| Field | Level | Example | Note

| term:mixs[DNA_sequence] | Required | CACCGCGGTTATACGAGAG… | Or provide the sequences in a fasta file.
| term:dwc[scientificName] | Strongly recommended | _Sprattus sprattus_ | The most specific identification, at any rank (see above), with authorship if you have it, or a BOLD BIN or UNITE SH code. No working names or qualifiers such as cf. or sp.
| term:dwc[kingdom] | Strongly recommended | Animalia | For eukaryotes, the kingdom: Animalia, Plantae, Fungi, Chromista or Protozoa. For bacteria and archaea, the domain: Bacteria or Archaea (there is no separate field for the domain). Helps GBIF match names that exist in more than one group.
| term:dwc[taxonRank] | Strongly recommended | species | The rank of the scientific name: domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus or species.
| term:dwc[phylum], term:dwc[class], term:dwc[order], term:dwc[family], term:dwc[genus] | Share if available | Chordata, Actinopteri, Clupeiformes, Clupeidae, _Sprattus_ | One column per rank, if they follow the <<gbif-taxonomy,GBIF taxonomy>>.
| term:dwc[verbatimIdentification] | Share if available | uncultured bacterium | The name from your reference database, if it is not a proper scientific name.
| term:dwc[scientificNameID] | Share if available | urn:lsid:marinespecies.org:taxname:137090 | For OBIS: the WoRMS identifier of the name.
| term:dwc[taxonConceptID] | Share if available | NCBI:txid9771 | For OBIS: the ID from your reference database, such as an NCBI taxon ID or a BOLD BIN.
|===

[[obis_data,names and identifiers for OBIS]]*Marine data for OBIS:* OBIS uses the WoRMS taxonomy, so add term:dwc[scientificNameID] and term:dwc[taxonConceptID] if your data should also reach OBIS (see the https://docs.gbif.org/publishing-dna-derived-data/en/#marine-datasets-and-the-ocean-biodiversity-information-system-obis[OBIS recommendations]). Two special cases:

* Sequences with no name at all: OBIS asks for the scientific name `Biota incertae sedis` with `urn:lsid:marinespecies.org:taxname:12` instead of an empty name. Do this only for data that should reach OBIS. GBIF reads it the same as an empty name.
* Names not in WoRMS, such as terrestrial species: give the name in term:dwc[scientificName] and leave term:dwc[scientificNameID] empty. GBIF matches the name as usual; OBIS cannot link it to WoRMS.
