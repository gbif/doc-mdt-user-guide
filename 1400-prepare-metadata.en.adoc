[[prepare_metadata]]
=== Prepare your metadata

The metadata describes your dataset: what it is, who made it and how. You enter it in step 5 (<<add_metadata>>), but it is easier to prepare the text beforehand.

Good metadata matters: the title and the creators become the dataset's citation, and the description decides whether others can find your data and trust it. You do not need a DOI: GBIF gives your dataset one when it is published.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| What to prepare | How to do it well

| *Title*
| Say what, where and which marker. For example: _"eDNA from water column to characterise fish and invertebrate communities from 30 sites in the Belgian Part of the North Sea – 12S"_ or _"COI data from: Environmental DNA metabarcoding differentiates between micro-habitats within the rocky intertidal"_.

| *Description*
| What the dataset contains, why it was collected, and how the samples were taken and processed. See this https://www.gbif.org/dataset/9358fbd7-cfd0-4eab-99fa-0934396a0529[well-described dataset on GBIF.org] for an example.

| *People*
a| * *Contact:* the person to ask about the dataset, with an email address.
* *Creators:* everyone who should be credited, in the order they should appear in the citation. Add ORCIDs where possible: GBIF registers the dataset's DOI with DataCite together with the creators' ORCIDs. If a creator has allowed DataCite to update their ORCID record, the dataset then appears automatically as a work in their ORCID profile.
* *Metadata providers:* people who wrote the documentation, if they are not creators.

To enter many people at once, fill in the https://mdt.gbif-test.org/templates/people.xlsx[people template] and upload it in step 5.

| *Licence*
a| * *CC0*: anyone may use the data without conditions.
* *CC-BY*: users must credit you.
* *CC-BY-NC*: users must credit you and may not use the data commercially. This limits reuse.

See https://www.gbif.org/terms[GBIF's data licences] and the https://ipt.gbif.org/manual/en/ipt/latest/applying-license[IPT guide to choosing a licence].

| *Methods*
| Sampling, sample preservation, DNA extraction, PCR and primers, sequencing platform, and bioinformatics: pipeline, denoising or clustering, and the reference database (with version) used for taxonomy.

| *Keywords*
| Words that help others find the dataset, for example the habitat, the target group and "eDNA".

| *Project*
| Project title and funding, and a website for the dataset or project, if any.

| *Citations*
| Papers based on the dataset. You can add papers that come out later by updating the dataset (see <<update>>).
|===

The MDT fills in the geographic, taxonomic and temporal coverage from your data. You only need to check it and tick the box that includes it in the metadata (see <<add_metadata>>).
